Indian Subsidiary Company Registration in India

Setting up an Indian subsidiary company is one of the most effective ways for foreign businesses to establish a legal presence in India. It operates as a separate legal entity under the Companies Act, 2013 , offering limited liability, tax advantages, and access to one of the world's fastest-growing markets.

What is Indian Subsidiary Company Registration & Why It Matters

The registration of a subsidiary company in India refers to the process of incorporating a company where a foreign parent company holds more than 50% of the total share capital. Under Section 2(87) of the Companies Act, 2013 , a subsidiary is defined as a company in which the holding company controls the composition of the Board of Directors or exercises majority voting rights.

Ongoing Compliance & Next Steps

Ensure continuous legal standing by maintaining the following post-registration requirements:

  • Corporate Tax Rate: 22% (for existing companies) or 15% (for new manufacturing companies) under the concessional tax regime
  • Dividend Distribution: Dividends paid to the foreign parent company are subject to withholding tax as per the applicable DTAA (Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement)
  • Transfer Pricing: All transactions between the parent company and subsidiary India must comply with Transfer Pricing regulations under the Income Tax Act
  • GST Registration : Mandatory if annual turnover exceeds the prescribed threshold
  • MAT (Minimum Alternate Tax): Applicable at 15% of book profits if regular tax liability is lower
  • Tax Incentives: Available for subsidiaries in SEZs, manufacturing sectors, and eligible startups
